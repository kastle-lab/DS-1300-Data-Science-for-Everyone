# DS 1300 : Data Science for Everyone

## Course Credit Hours : 4

## Motivation
Data science has become an important tool in both industry and academia and performs a wide variety of services: from actively transforming fields like manufacturing and healthcare or as a method for uncovering insights in the digital humanities by analyzing and visualizing real-world datasets. In this introductory undergraduate course, we will cover the fundamental concepts, techniques, and tools like Orange for navigating the entire data science lifecycle, as well as utilize these techniques to tackle real-world applications alongside domain experts.

## Required Background
A foundational understanding of college algebra is expected.

## Course Learning Objectives
In this course, students will:

* **Define** clear business objectives by translating real-world challenges into structured data science problem statements.
* **Gain** hands-on experience in gathering, cleaning, and visually exploring datasets to uncover hidden patterns and formulate hypotheses.
* **Explore** feature engineering and train predictive machine learning models to generate actionable predictions.
* **Acquire** practical skills in insights reporting and communicating analytical findings to stakeholders through interactive visualizations.
* **Understand** the fundamentals of Large Language Models (LLMs) and apply ethical frameworks for responsible AI usage.

## Course Learning Outcomes
Students who successfully complete this course can:

* **Achieve** proficiency in building visual data science pipelines using Orange without traditional coding.

* **Understand** and execute end-to-end data workflows, including data mining, cleaning, and visual exploration.

* **Apply** this knowledge to engineer features and implement supervised and unsupervised machine learning models.

* **Translate** technical model results into actionable business insights and compelling visual reports.

* **Evaluate** the ethical implications of AI and apply responsible practices when utilizing Large Language Models (LLMs).

## GenAI Usage
In this course, you are permitted to use Generative AI tools to assist with writing code or building data workflows in Orange; however, under no circumstances may you use GenAI to write, outline, or generate any portion of your written reports. All analysis, insights, and communication of findings must be entirely your own original work. If you choose to utilize AI for technical assistance, you bear full and ultimate responsibility for the output. You are expected to act ethically by critically evaluating, testing, and verifying any AI-generated code for accuracy and bias rather than blindly trusting the results, ensuring that all submitted work meets the highest standards of academic integrity.

## Academic Integrity
Discussion of course content with other students can be very useful to your learning and is encouraged. However, it is expected that work you turn in, your assignment, and your project will be completed on a purely individual basis. Students may discuss general concepts with one another, but may not, under any circumstances, work together on the actual implementation of any course assignment or the creation of report(s) turned into the instructor. Also, it should go without saying that quizzes and the final project should represent your individual effort. A good rule of thumb is this: If you’re having a discussion on basic concepts and your shared understanding of those concepts, you’re fine. If you’re actually writing report text and/or code in the presence of and with direct contribution from another person you’re not fine. If you are including text and/or code written by a third party (with the exception of specific support libraries approved by the instructor), you are definitely NOT fine. If you have ANY questions about what constitutes allowable collaboration or code library use, please contact the instructor for clarification. He will be happy to help you determine if your use of a specific library or package is acceptable or if your intended collaboration with another student crosses any ethical lines. In addition to the above policies related to collaboration stated in this syllabus, students are expected to at all times comply with the Wright State University Code of Student Conduct and in particular the portions pertaining to Academic Integrity.
